# Reading guide

Start with the [survey roadmap](figures/Figure_01_Survey_Roadmap.pdf), then use the route that matches your question. The roadmap labels S1–S7 refer to sections in the separate supplementary file.

## Choose a route through the paper

| If you want to understand | Read | Then check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hip, knee or ankle mobility during walking | §5 and S1–S3 | The joint, gait phase and any compensation at a neighbouring joint |
| What video can estimate | §6 and S4–S6 | The landmarks, camera view, output units and comparison method |
| What a benchmark can support | §6.5 and S7 | The target outcome, held-out subjects and reference measurement |
| Whether an estimate transfers to another setting | §7 | Occlusion in swing, loose clothing, changing illumination and the population studied |
| How fall-related patterns are assessed | §6.4 | The sensing modality, the reported endpoint and whether follow-up was used |

## Identify the clinical target

Figure 2 uses ankle motion during swing to connect a clinical variable to a video estimate and its reference. Specify the angle and phase first. Then ask whether the foot and shank landmarks are visible in the recording and whether the reference was collected under the same conditions. [Review axes](figures/Figure_02_Review_Axes.pdf)

## Check what was measured

Video-based outcomes may include step timing, joint angles, or the classification of a movement pattern. Each result should be read with its own outcome, error metric and test population. When comparing studies, check the level of analysis and whether the measurements use the same definitions and units.

## Read reliability claims carefully

Self-occlusion during swing can hide the foot or knee. Loose clothing can affect landmark localisation, while lighting can alter image quality during a recording. Read the robustness section with the camera layout and validation design in mind. Long periods with missing landmarks should be assessed separately from frames where the joint is clearly visible.

## Use the research priorities as testable questions

Choose a joint and an outcome, specify a reference, and test whether the result holds in the intended recording conditions and population. For a repeated assessment, report both estimation error and the ability to measure change across sessions.
